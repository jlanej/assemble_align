# copyproof: progress update, 16 November 2026

This update reviews `main` at the merge of PR #1 (commit `4edad6a`, 16 November 2026) against the
guide's validation ladder ([GUIDE.md](GUIDE.md)) and the review of 14 November. It says what was
built, what was measured, what changed since that review, and what to do next. Every number is
taken from the records under [experiments/](experiments/README.md) or from the
[README](README.md#what-it-finds), which link to their tables.

## Summary

- **Built.** A working prototype (0.1.0): a k-mer engine in Rust (`cpk`), a Python package with
  four checks (copy number, missing sequence, parental hapmers, an optional long-read audit),
  evidence cards and 38 tests. Re-run on the merged `main` for this update, all 38 pass (63 s,
  Python 3.11.17, macOS; none skipped). The tests are run by hand; there is no CI.
- **The go/no-go test passed by a wide margin.** On the T2T-HG002 Q100 truth genome, 23 of
  20,237 anchored duplication boundaries (0.11%) have an issue within 5 kb and 18 (0.09%) a
  high-confidence one. The target was at most 2%.
- **Precision on one person is about 95%.** On 13 HG002 assemblies scored against the truth,
  1,199 of 1,258 high-confidence copy-number calls are confirmed in twelve (95.3%). The share is
  the same before and after the rules stopped changing (95.7% and 94.7%, Fisher p = 0.41).
  Unconfirmed calls number 1 to 10 per assembly, and none of the 69 lies on a known issue of the
  truth genome.
- **The pre-registered held-out test on NA19240 holds on all seven comparisons.** It is a weak
  test: one person, two other assemblies standing in for a truth genome, and small counts for
  collapses.
- **Main gaps.** Precision has been measured on HG002 only, and collapses are found less often
  than surplus copies. Changes carried by part of the cells have no class, and the phase audit
  is not independent on trio-phased releases. The release scan has not started.
- **Next.** Freeze 0.1.0 at the thresholds NA19240 tested, and label alleles that part of the
  cells carry. Then run a pilot scan of 20 HPRC release-2 samples that includes rung 5. Build
  further only where the pilot shows the need.

## What was built

| Part | What it does | Where |
|---|---|---|
| k-mer engine `cpk` (Rust) | Counts every fourth canonical 31-mer, chosen by a fixed hash, in reads and assemblies; tracked counts are exact, except that a Bloom-filter false positive adds 1. Against jellyfish 2.3.1 on 4 million real reads, 13,534,071 of 13,534,369 tracked k-mers are identical and 297 are higher by one | `engine/`, [docs/method.md](docs/method.md) §1 |
| S1, copy number | A hidden Markov model over 1-kb windows compares each k-mer's count in the reads with its copies in the whole release, with a lookup of where the other copies lie: `COLLAPSE`, `EXPANSION`, `UNSUPPORTED`, `BASE_ERROR` | `src/copyproof/cn.py`, `copies.py` |
| S2, missing sequence | Solid read k-mers absent from the release, placed by the flanks of the reads that carry them: `MISSING_ALLELE`, `MISSING_JUNCTION`, `MISSING_SEQUENCE`, `MISSING_HAPLOTYPE` | `missing.py` |
| S3, parental hapmers | K-mers from one parent only: `PHASE_SWITCH`, `MISPLACED_PIECE` | `hapmers.py` |
| S4, long-read audit (optional) | Long reads at every run of N and every stretch no short read holds, fetched through a BAM index: `UNSUPPORTED_JOIN`, `FALSE_JOIN` | `joins.py` |
| Outputs | `issues.tsv`, `summary.json`, `run.json`, `model.json`, `cards/`, `cannot_assess.bed` (about 11% of a genome) | [docs/outputs.md](docs/outputs.md) |
| Tests | Engine against a naive counter; synthetic genomes with planted errors of each class | `tests/` |

On a 10-core, 64 GB laptop, counting 107 Gb of reads takes 5 minutes (30 from a CRAM) and 20 GB.
Scanning a diploid assembly takes 1.5 minutes, the checks after it about 7 to 20 minutes, and the
long-read audit 10 to 20 more ([README](README.md#what-it-costs)).

## Assessment

**What the evidence supports.** The false-flag rate on the truth genome is about 18 times below
the target, which makes a release scan worth doing. High-confidence copy-number calls are mostly
right on HG002, and the share did not fall once the rules stopped changing. Surplus copies of 5 kb
and more are found reliably, both in real assemblies (73–79%) and when planted (46 of 47, and 47
of 48 on NA19240). The records are careful: denominators were fixed in advance, the held-out test
was pushed before it was run, and every post-hoc rule is declared.

**What it does not yet support.**

1. *Precision on a new person.* NA19240 was judged against two other assemblies. On HG002 these
   confirm 62% to 85% of the calls that the truth confirms at 96%. The test can rule out a large
   drop but not a few points (surplus copies, p = 0.066).
2. *Collapses.* A collapse is one of the HG01943 example's errors, and collapses are found less
   often than surplus copies: 25–54% from 5 kb, against 73–79%.
3. *Phasing on trio-phased releases.* Here the phase audit reads the evidence the assembler used,
   so it can find only what went wrong after phasing.
4. *Changes in part of the cells.* They have no class, so the HG01103 case remains untested.

**A finding beyond the tool.** On the HPRC releases of HG01943 and NA19240, 32% and 52% of strong
missing-allele calls are alleles in neither parent, at read counts below one copy; on HG002 the
share is 7%. The long reads those releases were built from hold the allele beside the release's
sequence at 716 of 736 events, as a change in part of the cells would. These cell-line changes
are not assembly errors, but any read-based audit of cell-line assemblies will meet them. Before a
release scan they need a class and a wording of their own, or they will be counted as errors.

## Recommended next steps

The steps are ordered by the information each buys for the effort it takes. Most of the effort
goes into building and revising models rather than into scripted runs. So the steps that need a
new model come after the pilot scan, which shows whether they are needed.

1. **Freeze 0.1.0 at the values NA19240 tested.** They are the only thresholds validated out of
   sample. The two alternatives (`min_seg_class` 10, `margin` 2) were no worse there, which is no
   reason to switch.
   - In the same small pull request, change the wording for missing alleles in neither parent
     (calls are unchanged), and add CI: a GitHub Actions job that builds the engine and runs the
     38 tests.
   - Keep `scripts/eval_assemblies.py` as frozen, and report the `S1+S4` counts beside its
     output.
   - The three long-read audit changes go to 0.2. Each needs the truth genome's false-flag count
     again, and the audit is optional.
2. **Label alleles that part of the cells carry.** The rule uses evidence already computed: the
   allele is in neither parent's reads, its read count is below one copy and, where long reads
   exist, a share of them hold it. It is needed before any scan, because these alleles are a third
   to a half of the strong missing-allele calls on two releases. Every sample so far was examined
   when the question arose, so the pilot is the rule's first test.
3. **Pilot release scan, with rung 5 inside it.** Run HG00733, HG02818 and NA19036, plus about
   17 samples drawn at random from the 121 HPRC release-2 samples with both parents. Declare the
   draw's seed and the per-class estimates before the run.
   - *Rung 5 comes with it:* the seven comparisons of the NA19240 test, on the three dual
     samples. HG00733 and HG02818 have both parents sequenced and Strand-seq-phased HGSVC3
     assemblies. That gives the phase audit its only independent test, which so far rests on 13
     calls from one person. NA19036 has no parents in the set, and its HPRC assembly was phased
     with Hi-C: a second pipeline for S1 and S2.
   - *Adjudication:* the release's own long reads check every high-confidence call. A blind
     review of a small stratified sample of evidence cards calibrates that check.
   - *What it measures:* the frequent classes per haplotype, with confidence intervals. For rare
     classes, such as misplaced pieces, it gives an upper bound; the full scan gives the estimate.
   - *Cost:* by the per-step times in the README, about 2 hours of laptop time per trio sample,
     or about 40 hours in all.
4. **Let the pilot decide what to build.** If depth anomalies of the HG01103 kind are common,
   build the fractional-copy class (S6 of the guide) and run HG01103. Measure phasing errors on
   HGSVC3, whose assemblies were phased without the parents; about 30 of its samples in the 1000
   Genomes set have both parents sequenced. On trio-phased HPRC assemblies the phase audit is
   circular. Then run the full scan.
5. **Write-up.** Once the pilot is in, the results would support a methods preprint.

**Deferred.**

- *Collapses in sequence held 4 to 8 times.* One copy among 4 to 8 is a 12–25% depth change,
  near the limit of 30x short reads. Long-read QC already covers this class: HMM-Flagger's full
  HiFi track flags both of HG01943's missing-copy stretches. copyproof's distinct contribution is
  placement, joins, junctions and parent of origin. The limit is stated, and Flagger's calls can
  sit beside copyproof's.
- *The three long-read audit changes* wait for 0.2.

**Do now; each costs little.**

- Send the HG01943 case to HPRC as an issue. The producers can check it against data this work
  does not use, and their verdict is the best label available outside HG002.
- A second truth genome is not worth searching for now: none of HG002's grade with a curated
  list of known issues was found.
